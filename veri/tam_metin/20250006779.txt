<p>Background
</p>
<p>• NASA has historically based fire safety on prevention by limiting the materials in the pressurized 
volume to materials that pass the NASA flammability test (NASA-STD-6001 Test 1)
</p>
<p>• Planned lunar surface missions will be at higher oxygen concentrations in the crew cabin (56.5 
kPa (8.2 psia) and 34% oxygen)
</p>
<p>– This presents a substantially higher flammability risk which will be mitigated by materials controls
• The uncertainty of the impact of lunar gravity on material flammability limits has been identified 
</p>
<p>as a risk by the Human Landing System (HLS) program.
– The root of this concern lies in data from low gravity testing which showed enhanced flammability 
</p>
<p>for some materials at very low flow rates that exist in reduced gravity.
• The NASA Engineering Safety Center (NESC) assembled a team comprised of people including 
</p>
<p>the NESC, HLS, NASA GRC, NASA WSTF and academic collaborators.
• This team developed a method to establish a correction factor that could be applied to NASA Test 
</p>
<p>1 data.
</p>
<p>3</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Correction Factor Approach Rational
</p>
<p>• Despite concerns about applicability in reduced gravity, NASA Test 1 will remain the test method 
for material selection due to 
</p>
<p>– Long historical pedigree
– Impracticality of reduced gravity testing for material approval
</p>
<p>• To address the potential for gravitational effects, a method was sought to address the potential 
for enhanced flammability at low-gravity
</p>
<p>• Partial-g testing is currently limited to low-gravity aircraft (~25 s) and the NASA-GRC Zero-G drop 
tower centrifuge rig (5 s).
</p>
<p>• The drop tower facility is more available
• A protocol was developed to conduct similar tests in partial–g (NASA-GRC centrifuge) and in 1-g 
</p>
<p>at WSTF
• Both tests were limited to 5 s to allow comparison
• Both tests were upward/concurrent flammability tests
</p>

<p>Test Campaigns 
</p>
<p>• Both the WSTF and GRC test programs used the same ignitor
</p>
<p>• WSTF testing upward
</p>
<p>• GRC testing necessarily horizontal during ignition, becoming “upward” after the drop starts.
</p>
<p>• WSTF ignition was at a reduced velocity to enhance ignition and then switched to the test flow 
rate
</p>
<p>• GRC ignition was with slightly enhanced oxygen flow over ignition area which was switched off 
as the drop started
</p>
<p>• If the sample extinguished in 5 seconds it was a “pass”
</p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Conclusions
</p>
<p>• Some materials (cellulose and pmma) show a clear increased flammability at reduced gravity
</p>
<p>• Higher temperature materials (Nomex and polycarbonate) may show decreased flammability at 
lunar gravity conditions
</p>
<p>• The flammability difference between 35 cm/s and no flow may be a conservative estimate of the 
impact of lunar gravity buoyancy on material flammability.
</p>
<p>• These observations should be tested for other materials and ideally in long duration lunar gravity 
testing
</p>
