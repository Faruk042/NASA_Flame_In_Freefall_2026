<p>Page 1
</p>
<p>NASA relies on a 1-g test for screening material flammability for flight. 
</p>
<p>• Material ignited near bottom and flame spreads upward.
</p>
<p>• If a material passes this test, then it is considered safe for 
spaceflight. 
</p>
<p>• If material fails, its use is restricted in time, quantity, etc.
</p>
<p>This test has been a practical means to assess the great number of 
materials that have been screened. 
</p>
<p>However, microgravity research has suggested that normal gravity 
may not represent the most flammable condition for a material. 
</p>
<p>Theoretical and experimental results have demonstrated that some 
materials that will not burn in 1-g will burn in 0-g (with a low-speed 
flow) or in partial gravity (such as Lunar-g).
</p>
<p>Flame Spread in Microgravity over 2.2-cm-wide SIBAL Fabric.
</p>
<p> Left: Concurrent flow (comparable to 1-g upward spread). 
Right: Opposed flow (comparable to 1-g downward spread). 
</p>
<p>In microgravity, both concurrent and opposed-flow flame 
spread are possible, but on Earth, downward flame spread 
(opposed flow) cannot be achieved. 
</p>
<p>This demonstrates enhanced limits of flammability in 
microgravity, and suggests similar behavior at partial gravity.
</p>
<p>FM2 Background</p>
<p></p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Background Summary
</p>
<p>There is mounting evidence that if a material passes the NASA standard test it might fail 
at levels of gravity and flow below 1-g.
</p>
<p>Experiments using drop towers and aircraft have provided valuable information but run 
into limitations.
</p>
<p>Tests on the Moon will resolve some important questions and set the primary 
benchmark for future tests.
</p>
<p>Page 2</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>BPS replanning request
</p>
<p>BPS management requested that the FM2 experiment increase alignment with NASA’s 
exploration needs.
</p>
<p>Better alignment with NASA needs was previously identified as an opportunity in the 
Science Concept Review
</p>
<p>The GRC science team agrees that the best opportunity to directly address a stakeholder 
needs would be to test a material being studied in Phase 3 of the HLS and NESC material 
flammability effort.
</p>
<p>Page 3</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Current concept
</p>
<p>Test a material with an expected Lunar LOC of 24-26% and an expected oxygen 
consumption of 0.08 mol fraction
</p>
<p>Conduct four tests at 8.2 psi:
</p>
<p>1. Start at 37% should burn to threshold
2. Start at 33% should burn to threshold
3. Start at 29% should burn to threshold
4. Start at 25% not expected to ignite but would provide a margin of 
</p>
<p>safety if the limit was lower than expected.
</p>

<p>FM2 Objectives
</p>
<p>Page 8
</p>
<p>A. Conduct Lunar surface flammability tests that are important to NASA HLS (Human Landing System).
</p>
<p>B. Conduct test to assist in validation of the Phase 3 testing in support of HLS and NESC program; Find 
flammability limit of 1 material per flight at 8.2 psia upward burning.
</p>
<p>C. Evaluate the hypothesis that some materials burning in partial-g environments may be more 
flammable compared to 1-g.
</p>
<p>1. Obtain data on ignition, flame growth, extinction oxygen levels, and flame spread over solid 
materials in Lunar gravity and compare to 1-g results.
</p>
<p>2. Understand how partial gravity changes the solid-gas interactions.
</p>
<p>D. Provide a bridge between the current 1-g material screening method and actual behavior in reduced 
gravity.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Initial Chamber Conditions
</p>
<p>Each of the four tests requires its own chamber initial atmosphere as follows with venting to vacuum between tests:
</p>
<p>Test 1: 8.2 psia (+/- 0.3 psi), 37% O2 , balance nitrogen)
Test 2: 8.2 psia (+/- 0.3 psi), 33% O2 , balance nitrogen)
Test 3: 8.2 psia (+/- 0.3 psi), 29% O2 , balance nitrogen)
Test 4: 8.2 psia (+/- 0.3 psi), 25% O2 , balance nitrogen)
</p>
<p>Fuel and mounting
</p>
<p>Fabric sheets (Synergy Pro “Navy”) mounted between thin metal frames 
</p>
<p>Expose 2 cm (+/- 0.05 cm) x 20 cm (+/- 0.2 cm) of both sides of the sheet
</p>
<p>Ignition
</p>
<p>Hot wire igniter for each individual sample (currently ignites the bottom for upward burns, but could be changed)
</p>

<p>Suggested Approach
</p>
<p>Identify small number of candidate samples 
from Phase 3 matrix.
</p>
<p>Conduct tests at GRC in centrifuge and flow 
tunnel combined with WSTF testing to down 
select to one material.
</p>
<p>Conduct upward flammability tests with up to 4 
samples
</p>
<p>FM2 hardware does not support ignition 
approaches used at WSTF or in Centrifuge. (flow 
change, oxygen change) 
</p>
<p>Expect oxygen concentration to decrease by up 
to 0.08 mol fraction in a test.
</p>
<p>This will enable “scanning” to the limit allowing 
easier ignition since the atmosphere will be 
more flammable
</p>

<p>• Very frequent EVAs are required to meet mission goals. 100% O2, 4.3 psia (to bend fingers, arms).
• HLS, Lunar habitat and pressurized rover will nominally use 8.2 psia / 34% O2, 66% N2 to “engineer-out” risk of 
</p>
<p>decompression sickness.
• Control bands are expected to allow up to 37% O2
</p>
<p>NASA’s focus on exploration goals will dictate the required cabin atmosphere
</p>
<p>• Humans respond well to constant oxygen partial pressure (even at reduced total pressure), 
• Fire responds to total oxygen concentration
• Fire risk sets the maximum oxygen boundary
• Fire safe materials become increasingly difficult to find as the oxygen concentration increases.
</p>
