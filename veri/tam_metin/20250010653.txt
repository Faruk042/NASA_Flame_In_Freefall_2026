<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Lunar Combustion Investigation: Flammability Results from a Rotating Sounding Rocket
</p>
<p>Objectives
Demonstrate the feasibility of conducting material flammability experiments in partial-g on the spinning Blue Origin 
New Shepard vehicle which simulates Lunar gravity with centrifugal force
</p>
<p>Obtain data from the flight that will support other simulated Lunar-g experiments in drop towers and parabolic 
flight, actual Lunar-g experiments, and computer models.
</p>
<p>Outcome
The LUCI scientific objectives were achieved. Material flammability testing at partial-g is feasible using a spinning 
vehicle. Conducting large scale flammability testing would require additional programmatic support. This might be 
considered should more Lunar-g flights become available using New Shepard.</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Page 3
</p>
<p>Overview
</p>
<p>Conduct partial-g fire tests on the Blue Origin sounding rocket.
</p>
<p> 
</p>
<p>Hypothesis: some materials burning in partial-g are more flammable than on Earth. This has 
important implications for the current 1-g material screening method used by NASA. 
</p>
<p>Fuel samples burned in pairs; cameras and other sensors record flame characteristics.
</p>
<p>Oxygen limits for upward and downward spread at partial-g will be compared to 1-g values. 
</p>
<p>Measured flame characteristics in 1-g and partial-g will be compared to detailed model predictions. 
These comparisons will refine pressure-gravity scaling relations and will be applied to other g-levels.
</p>
<p> 
</p>
<p>The work directly addresses knowledge gaps in flammability and crew safety as defined in several 
NASA strategic documents.</p>
<p></p>

<p>Page 10
</p>
<p>The effective floor of our hardware (orientated so that the 
centrifugal g-vector points downward toward the floor) 
measures about 41 cm x 48 cm. The available height in the 
g-direction is about 53 cm. 
</p>
<p>NOTE: How we orient our samples depends on the specific 
locker location. It is required that the principle vertical 
axes of the burn samples be aligned with the radial lines. </p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>• Blue Origin New Shepard sounding rocket yields Lunar gravity by using centrifugal acceleration.
</p>
<p>• These are the first-ever, extended-duration (greater than 25 seconds) combustion tests performed in simulated Lunar gravity. 
</p>
<p>• A steady vehicle rotation of 11 RPM is established, with an effective radius of rotation of 1.22 m at the experiment location. 
</p>
<p>• Two samples are burned simultaneously in air at normal pressure: Cotton-fiberglass composite fabric sheet and a 4-mm diameter 
</p>
<p>rod of acrylic plastic (polymethyl methacrylate). 
</p>
<p>• Flame images from two orthogonal cameras are used to determine flame spread and growth rates, and the burn lasts for over 
</p>
<p>two and a half minutes. 
</p>
<p>• The oxygen concentration in the chamber is measured. As the burns progress, the oxygen concentration drops, enabling a 
</p>
<p>limiting value for extinction to be defined. 
</p>
<p>• The effect of Coriolis force is discussed. 
</p>
<p>• Results from this experiment are compared to related 1-g work, drop tower tests, aircraft experiments, and modeling. 
</p>
<p>• The results are applicable to NASA’s exploration efforts in that they guide the selection of fire-safe materials and environments 
</p>
<p>and ensure safety of future spacecraft and Lunar habitats. The data also provides fundamental understanding of fires. 
</p>
<p>• Finally, the experiment is a steppingstone to the ultimate goal of performing burn tests on the Lunar surface in the Flammability 
</p>
<p>of Materials on the Moon (FM2) experiment which is planned to launch in late 2026. </p>
<p></p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p> History
• ZIN technologies brought on board October 2021
</p>
<p>• Initial Planned launch was Spring 2023, Launch provider delay initially delayed launch into late Summer early Fall 2024, until 
it finally launched Feb 4 2025
</p>
<p>• First mission sequence test was performed in July 2023
</p>
<p>• Due to launch slip some improvements were made and last mission sequence test was ran in February 2024 before 
being shelved prior to flight. 
</p>
<p> Objectives
• Burn SIBAL fabric &amp; PMMA 
</p>
<p>• Conduct first-ever, long duration observation and measurement of flame spread in a partial-g environment
</p>
<p>• Verify this platform is a viable means to obtain material flammability data in Lunar-g and will include an assessment of 
the Coriolis force on results
</p>
<p>• Provide guidance for sample selection and test definition for a Lunar in-situ experiment
</p>
<p> Data
• First Flight
</p>
<p>• Measurements of flame spread rate and output of other diagnostics, including g-level vs. time for the two samples
</p>
<p>• Sample closeout photos and photos before and after each test
</p>
<p>• Data from 1-g burns</p>

<p> Potential Follow-on Objectives
• Evaluate the hypothesis that some materials burning in partial-g environments may be more flammable 
</p>
<p>compared to 1-g.
</p>
<p>• Compare upward and downward oxygen limits to 1-g values
</p>
<p>• Evaluate flame size, strength, intensity, and spread compared to 1-g
</p>
<p>• Develop a numerical model
</p>
<p>• Assess the applicability of the model by comparing to the test data obtained. 
</p>
<p>• Determine applicability of the pressure-gravity scaling relations for upward spread rates. 
</p>
<p>• Refine model using experiment results
</p>
<p>• Provide a bridge between current 1-g material screen method and actual behavior in reduced gravity.
</p>
<p> Data
• Flammability limits (%O2) for a subset of materials in Lunar-g
</p>
<p>• Measurements of the flame spread rate over materials in exploration atmospheres
</p>
<p>• Sample closeout photos, and photos before and after each test
</p>
<p>• Data from 1-g burns</p>

<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>The LUCI project achieved the first-ever, extended duration combustion tests (greater than 25 seconds) in simulated 
Lunar gravity. The results are applicable to NASA’s exploration efforts in that they guide the selection of fire-safe 
materials and environments and ensure safety of future spacecraft and Lunar habitats. The data also provides 
fundamental understanding of fires burning at reduced gravity for comparison to sophisticated numerical models. 
Finally, the experiment is a steppingstone to the ultimate goal of performing burn tests on the Lunar surface in the 
Flammability of Materials on the Moon (FM2) experiment which will fly on a SpaceX Human Lander System 
unmanned test flight. </p>

~constant preheat length.</p>
