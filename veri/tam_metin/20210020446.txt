Flammability Limit Definitions
♦ Differences in the flammability limit are even more important for spacecraft fire safety

• Upward Limiting Oxygen Index (ULOI)
‒ Oxygen concentration where half of the samples will pass NASA-STD-6001 Test 1

• Maximum Oxygen Concentration (MOC)
‒ Oxygen concentration where at least five samples pass Test 1 and were at least one sample failed in the 

environment that contained 1 percent more oxygen by volume

Electromotive Drop Tower
♦ Motivation

• Provide inexpensive, reliable and flexible access to 10 s of zero-g or partial-g to facilitate fundamental research, 
technology development, and flight hardware risk mitigation testing.

♦ Concept
• Convert the existing 5.2-second GRC Zero Gravity Research Facility to a state-of-the-art ~10-

second, 0 to 0.5g test facility using a vertical rail and linear synchronous motor system to provide a 
revolutionary capability in testing at low and partial gravity at a significantly lower operating cost.

♦ Target Metrics
• 10 s duration
• 10-5 g below 20 Hz
• Launch/landing 4 g avg 6 g max
• Payload size 1 m x 1 m x 1.8 m
• Payload mass 455 kg
• 20 tests / day (1 OOM increase)
• $300 / test (1 OOM reduction)

Conclusions

♦ Owing to lack of access to partial gravity test platforms, the data set of lunar flammability tests is 
very small

♦ Theory and experimental results indicate that oxygen flammability limits in lunar-g are lower than 
those measured in 1-g
• The magnitude of ∆%O2 depends not only on the material but on the direction of burning (concurrent or 

opposed)

♦ Configurations that will not support a flame in low-gravity could support a flame in lunar-gravity

♦ The %O2 of planned habitat atmosphere exacerbates these issues by enhancing the flammability

♦ Additional work is needed to establish a partial gravity flammability acceptance criterion

♦ Lunar gravity can change fire detection strategy depending on vehicle design

♦ Turning the ECLSS flow off to extinguish a fire in lunar-g is not effective because the reduced gravity 
will still induce buoyant flow velocities

♦ We are developing a plan to quantify the magnitude of ∆%O2 using a combination of modeling, low-
gravity aircraft, drop tower centrifuge, and rotating capsule in a sub-orbital flight to be anchored by a 
lunar lander test 17
